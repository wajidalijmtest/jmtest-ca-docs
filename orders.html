1The whole flow

Customer side, in apps/web

The customer pays, and the order is saved in one database transaction together with an outbox row. Everything the customer sees happens here. Business Central is not called during checkout at all, so a Business Central outage never blocks a sale.

apps/web and services Postgres Hand-off to the sync job
The sales team email means “paid”, not “in Business Central”.It is sent before the sync runs. A separate alert (§3) tells the team when an order could not be created in Business Central.

2Sync to Business Central

jobs/integration-outbox

The outbox job picks up pending order.confirmed rows and creates the Sales Quote. Every step is safe to repeat, so a retry after a timeout never creates a second customer or a second quote.

3When the sync fails

Retry, then roll back and tell people

The order is already paid and saved, so a failed sync never loses it. The job retries errors that can recover by themselves, and stops straight away on errors that cannot. Either way, after the last attempt the sales team is told and the order shows up in the CMS with a Retry button.

Which errors are retried

ResponseTypeWhat the job does
timeout, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504TemporaryRetry with backoff. For 429, wait at least the Retry-After time.
401Temporary, onceGet a new token and retry straight away. A second 401 is permanent: the secret or app registration is wrong, so IT is alerted.
412Temporary, onceRead the record again for a fresh etag and retry.
400, 404PermanentStop. The data is wrong, for example a SKU with no BC item number. Retrying gives the same error.
403PermanentStop and alert IT. The app is missing permissions in Business Central.

Retry schedule

Failureattempts afterNext try
1st1in 1 minute
2nd2in 5 minutes
3rd3in 15 minutes
4th4in 30 minutes
5th5No retry. Roll back, mark failed, notify.

Total wait before the sales team hears about a temporary problem: about 51 minutes. A permanent error alerts them on the first attempt.

4Step by step

{base} is https://api.businesscentral.dynamics.com/v2.0/{tenantId}/{environment}/api/v2.0/companies({companyId}). Every BC call sends Authorization: Bearer {access_token}. Values in the examples are illustrative.

Checkout

apps/web, while the customer waits

  1. C1
    APPBuild the cart and choose pickupcommerce.cart_items

    The customer adds products and picks a pickup location: Edmonton, Richmond or Calgary. A calibration add-on is its own cart line with line_type = 'calibration' and parent_line_id pointing at the product line; its quantity always equals the parent's.

    Rental lines cannot be bought directly. A cart with a rental line is sent as an RFQ instead, and checkout/intent returns 409 if one gets through.

  2. C2
    APPCalculate tax with Avalarasubtotal + freight + handling

    The storefront sends the cart to AvaTax as a SalesOrder estimate, with the pickup branch as the address and freight and handling as separate lines. AvaTax returns the tax the customer pays. A new estimate is requested whenever the cart or pickup location changes. Full detail in §7.

  3. C3
    APPTake payment with Stripecheckout/intent → Stripe PaymentIntent, CAD

    The order is saved only once Stripe confirms the payment, through the payment_intent.succeeded webhook. Handle that webhook idempotently: Stripe can send it more than once, and the outbox unique key (topic, aggregate_id) stops a second outbox row.

    Once the payment succeeds, record the tax in Avalara with createoradjust, type: SalesInvoice, commit: true and the order number as the code (§7).

  4. C4
    APPSave order and outbox row in one transactioncommerce.orders · order_items · integration_outbox

    All three writes commit together or not at all. That is what guarantees every paid order reaches the sync job.

Sync to Business Central

jobs/integration-outbox, in the background

  1. S1
    SQLClaim pending rowscommerce.integration_outbox

    The claim pushes next_attempt_at forward as a short lease, so two job runs never work on the same order and a crashed run's rows come back by themselves after five minutes.

    Query

    UPDATE commerce.integration_outbox o
SET    next_attempt_at = now() + interval '5 minutes'
WHERE  o.id IN (
  SELECT id FROM commerce.integration_outbox
  WHERE  topic = 'order.confirmed'
    AND  status = 'pending'
    AND  next_attempt_at <= now()
  ORDER  BY created_at
  LIMIT  10
  FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
)
RETURNING o.*;

6Open decisions

Need an answer before build

Sales Quote or Sales Order?

The data model sends order.confirmed as a Sales Quote. But the customer has paid, so it is a confirmed sale, and a quote has to be converted by hand.

SuggestCreate a Sales Order with POST /salesOrders (same shape), or call makeOrder right after S6.

Whose tax wins?

Avalara calculates what the customer pays; Business Central calculates its own tax on the quote. If they differ, the BC document won't match the Stripe charge.

SuggestTreat Avalara as the source and line up BC tax areas, or use the Avalara connector for Business Central. Keep the S6 check either way.

Checkout when Avalara is down

Without an AvaTax estimate the total can’t be shown correctly. Either checkout pauses, or it uses a stored rate per branch and corrects it later.

SuggestPause checkout with a clear message. Outages are rare and short, and a wrong tax charge is harder to fix than a short delay.

Tax codes

Products, calibration services, freight and handling each need the right Avalara tax code, or taxability is decided wrongly.

SuggestFinance confirms the codes; store the product code per SKU in the catalog so the storefront sends it on every line.

Pickup location in BC

Edmonton, Richmond and Calgary need to land somewhere the warehouse looks.

SuggestMap each to a BC location code and a PICKUP shipment method. Confirm with the BC admin which fields the warehouse uses.

Recording the Stripe payment

The payment is taken in Stripe, but Business Central doesn't know it was paid.

SuggestAgree with finance how Stripe payouts are matched: a cash receipt per order, or a daily Stripe payout entry.

7How tax is calculated

Avalara AvaTax, in real time at checkout

The storefront never calculates tax itself. At checkout it sends the order to AvaTax, and AvaTax works out the tax for every line and every jurisdiction in one call, usually in well under a second. The amount AvaTax returns is the amount the customer pays, so Avalara is the source of truth for tax on jmtest.ca orders.

What AvaTax does with each request

  1. 1

    Takes the inputs

    Addresses, date, customer, and the line items, with freight and handling as their own lines.

    jmtest.ca: the pickup branch address, the order date, the customer’s email as the customer code, and every cart line.

  2. 2

    Validates the location

    Checks the address well enough to find the right taxing jurisdiction.

    jmtest.ca: the three branch addresses are fixed, so they validate the same way every time.

  3. 3

    Applies sourcing rules

    Decides where the sale legally takes place for tax purposes.

    jmtest.ca: pickup means the sale happens at the branch, so the branch’s province decides the tax, not the customer’s home address.

  4. 4

    Decides taxability per line

    Uses each line’s tax code and local rules to decide whether, and how, it is taxed.

    jmtest.ca: products, calibration services, freight and handling each get their own tax code.

  5. 5

    Checks exemptions

    Removes tax the buyer doesn’t owe, when exemption details are sent.

    jmtest.ca: only for customers with a certificate on file in Avalara. Everyone else is taxed normally.

  6. 6

    Calculates and returns

    Applies the rate for each jurisdiction and tax type, and returns a breakdown. In Canada that covers GST, HST, PST and QST.

    jmtest.ca: the storefront shows the total tax; the breakdown is kept on the order.

  7. 7

    Records the decision

    Saves the transaction for reporting and audit, if the request asks it to.

    jmtest.ca: only after payment succeeds. Checkout estimates are never recorded.

When the storefront calls AvaTax

Two kinds of call, at two moments. The difference is whether AvaTax keeps a record.

MomentCallTypeRecorded in Avalara?
Cart or pickup location changes at checkoutPOST /api/v2/transactions/createSalesOrderNo. An estimate to show the customer; call it as often as needed.
Stripe confirms the paymentPOST /api/v2/transactions/createoradjustSalesInvoice, commit: trueYes. This is the record Avalara uses for filing and audit.

The recorded call uses the order number as its code. createoradjust updates the existing transaction if one with that code already exists, so a repeated Stripe webhook or a retry never records the same sale twice.

Which tax applies at each pickup branch

Because the sale happens at the branch, the branch’s province sets the tax. AvaTax applies the real rates; the figures below are the current general rates, shown so the totals make sense.

Pickup branchProvinceTaxes on a taxable product
EdmontonAlbertaGST 5%. Alberta has no provincial sales tax.
CalgaryAlbertaGST 5%.
RichmondBritish ColumbiaGST 5% + BC PST 7%.

The same cart therefore costs more in Richmond than in Edmonton. Changing the pickup location at checkout must trigger a new AvaTax estimate.

Why freight and handling are separate linesTheir taxability can differ from the products they come with, and it varies by province. Sent as their own lines with their own tax codes, AvaTax taxes each one correctly. Folded into a product price, they would be taxed as if they were the product.

Exempt customers

Some business customers don’t pay certain taxes. Their exemption certificates live in Avalara, linked to their customerCode, so the same customer code must be used on every call. When AvaTax finds a valid certificate, it removes the exempt tax in step 5 and shows the reason in the response. The storefront doesn’t decide exemptions itself.

If Avalara is unavailable

Call failsWhat happens
The checkout estimateThe customer can’t be shown a correct total, so payment isn’t taken. Checkout shows a “try again” message. Whether to fall back to a stored rate instead is an open decision, §6.
The record after paymentThe order is already paid and saved, so nothing is lost. The call is retried the same way as the Business Central sync, through an outbox row with the proposed topic order.tax_commit.
Avalara and Business Central must agreeThe customer pays the AvaTax amount. Business Central calculates tax again on the quote (§2, step S6), and the two can differ if the BC tax setup doesn’t match. That check flags any order where they disagree.
