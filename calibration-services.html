1The customer journey

From first visit to the next calibration

Four stages. The customer starts on the calibration page, gets a quote, sends the equipment or books an onsite visit, receives the calibrated equipment with its paperwork, and then manages everything in the Customer Portal until the next calibration is due, which starts the cycle again.

3In-lab or onsite

Two ways to get equipment calibrated

  • In-lab

    Equipment goes to the lab

    The equipment is calibrated at one of JM Test’s accredited labs. The customer ships it with the RMA form, or uses pickup and delivery where it’s available.

    Best for: a few instruments, or equipment that can be out of service for a while.

  • Onsite

    The lab comes to the customer

    Calibration at the customer’s site, or in a mobile lab. Onsite calibration has its own page with more detail.

    Best for: many instruments, equipment that can’t be moved, or minimum downtime.

Whichever the customer picks, the calibration, the paperwork and the portal afterwards are the same.

4What each calibration includes

Standard with every calibration

  • 1

    Traceable calibration certificate

    Includes the readings before and after adjustment: the as-found and as-left data. As-found shows whether the instrument was out of tolerance before calibration.

  • 2

    Calibration sticker and seals

    A sticker on the instrument showing its calibration status, plus tamper seals where they apply.

  • 3

    Basic cleaning

    The instrument is given a basic clean as part of the service.

  • 4

    Records kept indefinitely

    Calibration history is never deleted, so the customer can always show an auditor past results.

  • 5

    Reminder when the next calibration is due

    A notice goes out ahead of the due date, which starts the next cycle of the journey.

5After the service

Customer Portal, jmtest.com/portal

The portal is open 24/7. It’s where the customer manages their calibrated equipment between visits.

  • Intervals and due dates

    See each instrument’s calibration interval and when it’s next due.

  • Print calibration reports

    Download and print certificates and reports, for audits or records.

  • Equipment status

    See where each instrument is and what state it’s in.

  • Due-date reminders

    Set up reminders for when calibration is coming due.

6Accreditation and coverage

What the lab is accredited for and what it calibrates

StandardWhat it means for the customer
ISO/IEC 17025, accredited by A2LAThe lab’s competence has been independently assessed. Certificates are downloadable from the calibration page.
Traceable to NISTMeasurements trace back to US national standards.
ANSI/NCSL Z540-1Meets a common US standard for calibration systems, often required by defense and aerospace customers.
ISO 10012-1Meets the international standard for measurement management.

Equipment covered

  • Pressure and vacuumaccredited to 30,000 PSI
  • Electronic
  • Fiber optic
  • Temperature
  • RF and microwave
  • Bio-medical
  • Electrical
  • Mechanical and dimensionalincl. torque, force, mass

Procedures followed

Each calibration follows one of three sources: the manufacturer’s procedure, a US Department of Defense (DoD) procedure, or JM Test’s own procedure.
